completed · 10m 24s
Both balloon-pods and proactive-nodes strategies absorbed a planned traffic surge in under 90 seconds. Balloon-pods were slightly faster (58s vs 1m 3s) and required less lead time. Neither required new EC2 provisioning during the surge itself.

Implication: For workloads needing all N nodes before serving traffic, the effective provision time is the slowest node (4m 26s here). The 5s fast path for nodes 1 & 2 reflects warm EC2 capacity from earlier test runs in this session — in a cold environment, all 3 nodes would likely take 4–5 minutes. Balloon pods or proactive node pre-warming eliminate this variance.

Key Findings

  • Karpenter scheduling decisions are fast: NodeClaim latency is consistently 1–8 seconds across all scenarios — the bottleneck is EC2 boot time (~4–5 min), not the scheduler.
  • Karpenter cold-start is ~5m 30s — faster than typical CAS (7–10 min) but still too slow for sudden spikes without balloon pods.
  • Consolidation is dramatically faster with Karpenter: 34s to first NodeClaim removal vs CAS's 10-minute minimum unneeded-time.
  • Balloon pods are essential even with Karpenter: Test 12 showed a 6m 34s degradation window without preparation vs 1m 3s with balloon pods — an 84% improvement.
  • Planned surges are well-handled by both strategies: Balloon-pods (58s) and proactive-nodes (1m 3s) both absorbed surges comfortably.
  • Parallel provisioning is mixed-path: Test 13 showed 2/3 nodes from warm EC2 capacity (5s) and 1/3 from cold boot (4m 26s), resulting in a 4m 20s stagger between nodes.
