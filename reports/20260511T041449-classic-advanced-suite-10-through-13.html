Key findings

Proactive design eliminates the EC2 provisioning penalty

Whether the spike is planned (test 11) or sudden (test 12), balloon pods or pre-warmed nodes reduced time-to-all-ready from 7–10 minutes to 31–43 seconds. The remaining latency is the HPA metrics-server detection window (~18–49s) — an irreducible baseline that requires KEDA or custom metrics to reduce further. Autoscaling design matters more than autoscaler choice.

Test 11 — Planned surge

Balloon-pods preparation is near-zero cost (<1s — pods cached) but surge response is 2m18s due to the HPA metrics window + preemption time. Proactive-nodes requires 6m23s lead time (CAS provisioning) but delivers a 43s surge response. Choose by how much advance notice you have: balloon-pods for uncertain timing, proactive-nodes for known events (retail sales, launches) where 10 min warning is feasible.

Test 12 — Sudden spike, 95% improvement

Phase 1 (raw CAS): EC2 provisioning dominated at 9m37s node ready time. Phase 2 (balloon pods): preemption completed in 20s, all pods Ready in 31s. The 95% improvement (9m48s → 31s) confirms balloon pods are highly effective for sudden spikes even on Classic CAS clusters. The remaining latency is the metrics-server window, not node provisioning.

Test 13 — CAS parallel provisioning

CAS dispatched parallel scale-ups to separate AZ MachineSets, achieving only 17s stagger between nodes 1 and 2 — well within the 30–90s documented range. This confirms CAS batches multi-AZ launches concurrently. Only 2/3 target nodes provisioned because demand was satisfied after 2 nodes; the trigger workload was not sufficient to force a 3rd CAS cycle. This is correct CAS behavior.

Test 10 — Karpenter (skipped)

Test 10 requires a ROSA HCP cluster with AutoNode (Karpenter) enabled. On Classic, CAS uses AWS Auto Scaling Groups and cannot create NodeClaims. To benchmark Karpenter NodeClaim latency, provision an hcp-autonode cluster and run this suite again. Karpenter typically achieves first-node-ready in 60–90s via EC2 Fleet API, compared to 7m36s CAS above.

Sizing note — balloon replicas

Balloon replicas should equal expected HPA target − current replicas. For this cluster (m5.xlarge, 3 workers), 5 balloon pods at 500m/128Mi each consumed ~2.5 vCPU of headroom across the 3 nodes — enough to absorb a 1→6 replica surge without CAS. For larger surges, combine balloon pods with proactive-nodes or increase the machine pool max-replicas before the event.
